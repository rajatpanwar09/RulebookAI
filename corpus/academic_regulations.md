\# University Academic Regulations



\## AR-1.1 Academic Year



The academic year is divided into two regular semesters and one optional summer term. The university publishes the academic calendar before the beginning of each academic year. Students are responsible for checking the calendar, registration announcements, examination notices, and departmental communications relevant to their programmes.



\## AR-1.2 Semester Registration



Students must complete semester registration within the registration period announced by the university. Registration normally requires the student to have cleared applicable academic and administrative holds. A student who fails to complete registration may be prevented from attending classes or appearing for assessments until the registration issue is resolved.



\## AR-1.3 Course Selection



Students must select courses appropriate to their programme, semester, and prerequisite requirements. A course may be added only when the student satisfies the published prerequisites or receives an approved exception from the competent academic authority.



\## AR-1.4 Academic Advising



Students should consult their academic adviser before making significant changes to their course registration. Academic advisers may recommend courses, explain programme requirements, and identify prerequisite or credit-load issues. Final approval remains subject to university regulations and the authority assigned to the relevant department.



\## AR-2.1 Credit Requirements



Students must complete the minimum number of credits prescribed by their programme to qualify for graduation. Credits earned through approved transfer, exchange, or other recognized academic arrangements may count toward graduation subject to university rules.



\## AR-2.2 Minimum Academic Performance



Students must maintain the minimum academic performance required by their programme. Academic performance may be evaluated using semester grade point average, cumulative grade point average, failed courses, academic warnings, or other measures specified by the programme.



\## AR-2.3 Academic Warning



A student whose academic performance falls below the prescribed standard may be placed on academic warning. The university may require the student to meet an adviser, reduce the course load, repeat courses, or follow an academic improvement plan.



\## AR-2.4 Academic Probation



A student who continues to fall below the required academic standard may be placed on academic probation. Probation conditions must be communicated through the student's department or academic office. Failure to satisfy probation requirements may result in restrictions on registration or further academic action.



\## AR-3.1 Course Attendance



Students are expected to attend scheduled lectures, tutorials, laboratories, practical sessions, seminars, and other compulsory academic activities. Departments may prescribe additional attendance requirements for courses involving laboratory or practical work.



\## AR-3.2 Attendance Recording



Attendance must be recorded using the method approved by the department or instructor. Students should verify their attendance record periodically and report an apparent recording error to the instructor within the period specified by the course or department.



\## AR-3.3 Attendance Shortage



A student whose attendance falls below the required level may receive an attendance shortage notice. The notice should identify the affected course and, where available, the number or percentage of missed sessions.



\## AR-3.4 Attendance Disputes



A student who believes that an attendance record is incorrect may submit supporting information to the course instructor. If the matter is not resolved, the student may approach the departmental academic office according to the grievance procedure.



\## AR-4.1 Examination Eligibility



A student must satisfy academic, registration, and attendance requirements before appearing in a semester examination. The examination office may withhold an admit card when a mandatory eligibility requirement has not been satisfied.



\## AR-4.2 Ordinary Attendance Threshold



For ordinary examination eligibility, a student must have at least 75 percent attendance in the relevant course. Attendance is calculated using the university's approved attendance records for scheduled compulsory academic sessions.



\## AR-4.3 Attendance Calculation



Attendance percentage is calculated as the number of approved attended sessions divided by the number of scheduled compulsory sessions, multiplied by 100. Authorized exclusions from the attendance calculation must be supported by appropriate records.



\## AR-4.4 Attendance Review



Before finalizing examination eligibility, departments should review attendance records and identify students below the ordinary attendance threshold. Students should be given an opportunity to report genuine recording errors before the final eligibility list is published.



\## AR-4.5 Attendance Shortage Consequences



A student who does not satisfy the ordinary attendance requirement may be declared ineligible for the relevant examination unless an approved exception applies. The examination office may withhold the examination admit card for an ineligible student.



\## AR-4.6 Approved Academic Activities



Participation in officially approved academic activities may be treated according to the attendance rules applicable to the activity. Examples may include university competitions, academic conferences, approved field visits, and officially assigned departmental activities.



\## AR-4.7 Attendance Waiver Authority



The academic authority designated by the university may consider attendance-related representations when exceptional circumstances are documented. Any waiver must be recorded through the prescribed institutional process.



\## AR-4.8 Medical Attendance Exception



A student who has a documented and officially approved medical absence may be permitted to appear for an examination with attendance as low as 60 percent in the affected course. The medical documentation must be submitted through the prescribed process and approved by the competent authority before the exception is applied.



\## AR-5.1 Examination Registration



Students must register for semester examinations within the examination registration period. Registration requires the student to satisfy applicable academic and administrative requirements.



\## AR-5.2 Examination Form



The examination form must contain accurate course, student, and programme information. A student is responsible for checking the submitted form and reporting errors before the published correction deadline.



\## AR-5.3 Examination Admit Card



An eligible student receives an examination admit card after successful completion of required examination registration. Students should carry the admit card and the identification document specified by the examination office.



\## AR-5.4 Examination Conduct



Students must follow examination-room instructions. Unauthorized materials, communication devices, or communication with another candidate may be treated as examination misconduct.



\## AR-5.5 Examination Absence



A student who does not appear for an examination receives an absence for that examination unless the examination office approves a recognized exception under the applicable regulations.



\## AR-5.6 Medical Examination Absence



A student unable to attend an examination because of a medical emergency may submit medical documentation according to the examination office procedure. Approval of the request is subject to verification and the authority assigned by the university.



\## AR-6.1 Assessment Components



Courses may use examinations, quizzes, assignments, projects, laboratory work, presentations, attendance, or other approved assessment components. The course outline should communicate the assessment structure to students.



\## AR-6.2 Internal Assessment



Internal assessment marks should be recorded according to the course assessment scheme. Students should be informed of their recorded marks through the mechanism specified by the department.



\## AR-6.3 Assignment Submission



Assignments must be submitted through the designated platform or method by the stated deadline. Late submission is governed by the course policy unless an approved exception applies.



\## AR-6.4 Academic Integrity



Students must submit original academic work and properly acknowledge sources. Plagiarism, unauthorized collaboration, fabrication, falsification, and other forms of academic dishonesty may be referred for disciplinary action.



\## AR-6.5 Grade Review



A student who believes that a grade contains an evaluation or recording error may request a review through the prescribed process. A grade review does not guarantee a change in marks.



\## AR-7.1 Supplementary Examination Attempts



A student may take a maximum of two supplementary examination attempts for an eligible failed course. The attempts are counted according to the examination records maintained by the university.



\## AR-7.2 Supplementary Eligibility



Eligibility for a supplementary examination depends on the student's academic status, the course's supplementary availability, and any restrictions published by the examination office.



\## AR-7.3 Supplementary Registration



Students must register for supplementary examinations within the supplementary registration period. Failure to register within the prescribed period may result in loss of that supplementary opportunity.



\## AR-7.4 Supplementary Result



The supplementary examination result is recorded according to the grading rules applicable to the course. The university may apply grade limits or other restrictions where expressly provided by programme regulations.



\## AR-8.1 Academic Appeals



A student may submit an academic appeal where an appeal mechanism is expressly provided by the applicable regulation. Appeals must normally identify the decision being challenged and provide relevant supporting information.



\## AR-8.2 Appeal Deadline



An appeal must be submitted within the deadline communicated by the relevant academic office. Appeals submitted after the deadline may be rejected unless the competent authority permits late submission.



\## AR-8.3 Appeal Decision



The designated academic authority reviews an appeal according to the applicable procedure. The decision communicated after review is final unless another appeal or review mechanism is expressly available.



\## AR-9.1 Graduation Eligibility



A student may graduate after completing all required credits, programme requirements, examination requirements, and administrative clearances. The university verifies graduation eligibility before issuing final graduation approval.



\## AR-9.2 Clearance Requirements



Students seeking graduation must obtain the clearances required by relevant university offices. These may include library, laboratory, finance, residence, and departmental clearances.



\## AR-9.3 Graduation Application



A graduation application must be submitted during the period announced by the university. The student is responsible for providing accurate information and completing any required supporting documentation.



\## AR-10.1 Student Records



Academic records are maintained by authorized university offices. Students should promptly report an apparent error in an official academic record through the designated process.



\## AR-10.2 Record Corrections



Corrections to official academic records require supporting evidence and approval from the authority responsible for the relevant record. Informal requests do not automatically change an official record.



\## AR-10.3 Communication



University notices may be communicated through official email, student portals, departmental notices, or other recognized channels. Students are expected to monitor official communications relevant to their academic obligations.
